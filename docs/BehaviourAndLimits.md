# Behaviour and limits

> SoftPoke 1.0.0 | [Index](Index.md)

## What SoftPoke computes

* **A baked response.** For each body region the bake tool builds a finite-element model of skin, fat and muscle, and compresses its response into a table. At run time the GPU looks up that table for the contact between the hand and the skin; it does not run a full simulation per frame. The model is linear (small deformation), nearly incompressible, with friction.
* **Hand contact.** The hand is a set of capsules built from the 21 tracked joints (a palm made of many capsules, three per finger, three for the thumb). Per-finger radii are fixed (about 10.5 / 9.5 / 8.5 mm along a finger at a reference hand size) and scale with the tracked hand size.
* **The "physics hand" limiter.** A tracked hand can go anywhere, including through the character. The limiter gives the solver a *proxy hand* that never sinks deeper into the skin than the local tissue thickness allows, and glides back to the tracked hand when you pull out. It is a **tuned look-and-feel limiter, not a physical constant**: the values (`ThicknessFraction`, `MaxDepthMm` and so on in `FSoftPokeLimiterSettings`) were chosen so that normal grips are not limited and extreme ones stay sane.
* **Stylize ("mochi" look).** An optional layer (`StylizeStrength`, on at 1 by default) raises a soft rim around the hand and ridges between the fingers. At 0 you get the pure physical result.
* **Jiggle.** An optional layer of soft-tissue vibration modes: it makes the tissue wobble after a release or a slap, and when the bone moves. Off with `bJiggle` or `JiggleAmount` = 0.
* **Bend following.** The baked regions follow all the bones that skin them, so a bent arm still deforms correctly.
* **Contact ripple.** A push, a release or a knead makes the touched region wave, a wide soft bulge rises around a dent, and (optional) the wave crosses region borders. It is built on the jiggle layer, so it needs `bJiggle`. Honest sizes. The physical wave of the baked model is tiny: the skin 15 to 20 cm (along the skin) from a 15 mm palm press moves a median of 0.01 mm and 0.05 to 0.2 mm at the 90th percentile. The default build therefore adds a stylised, display-only wave (`bRippleWave`): the low modes of the region are kicked by the rate of the contact load, the wave field is normalised so that it has the same size everywhere in the region and it dies off exponentially beyond 8 cm (`RippleWaveReachCm`). Measured along the local skin normal on the sample body (palm press 1.5 cm, 60 fps, 15 to 20 cm geodesic distance from the touch, peak over the stroke, median / 90th percentile / maximum over the skin points of the ring): thigh 0.7 / 1.7 / 3.5 mm, upper arm 0.5 / 1.6 / 2.8 mm, belly 1.9 / 2.6 / 3.9 mm, chest 1.7 / 3.7 / 5.1 mm (chest and belly include the bulge), with 52 to 90 % of the ring points moving by more than 0.5 mm. That is 2 to 4 mm at most places in the first second after a push or a release and a few tenths of a millimetre afterwards: about 1 to 3 pixels on a medium shot at 720p, so it is visible as a moving shading and silhouette in a video, not in a still image. One to three cycles are visible after a release (5 to 8 Hz) and the region is at rest after about 1.0 s (below 0.05 mm). A steady hold does not move at all (the wave follows the rate of the contact load). A kneading hand (2.5 Hz) gives a large kick at the first contact and a ringing release, but in between the far skin moves much less: after the first second the amplitude at 15 to 20 cm is a median of 0.05 mm (thigh), 0.15 (upper arm), 0.08 (belly) and 0.11 mm (chest) and a 90th percentile of 0.22, 0.55, 0.15 and 0.30 mm, flat over a 3 to 5 minute soak (no drift, no NaN). The wave follows the rate of the load change and slow kneading is a nearly quasi-static motion of the tissue; there is no stylised permanent wobble. The 3 to 5 mm peaks above belong to the first contact and the release. `RippleWaveGain` 0 gives the physical wave only; the gain is bounded (a state limit and `RippleWaveMaxMm`), so 3 gives only about 1.7 times the motion of 1 on the thigh (median 0.7 to 1.2 mm). The default bulge (`RippleBulgeFraction` 3, `RippleBulgeMaxMm` 3, near 40 mm, far 80 mm) raises the mean height of the ring 3 to 8 cm from the contact by 0.6 mm (thigh), 0.5 mm (upper arm) and 0.8 mm (chest) over the stylize rim; the belly gets nothing (stiff, thin: the dent is 4 mm). With the knobs at their maximum (`RippleBulgeFraction` 8, `RippleBulgeMaxMm` 5) the thigh gains about 2 mm and the chest 1.5 mm. Details and limits:
  * The wave and the bulge never feed the contact solve: the depth of the dent at the touch is identical with the ripple on or off (the peak differs by at most 0.02 mm at the defaults in the palm-press measurements). The wave starts 2 cm from a contact centre and is at full size at 5 cm, so in single frames at the press onset the skin 2 to 3 cm from the centre can differ by up to 0.7 mm (thigh, upper arm; the run-to-run noise floor is 0.1 to 0.6 mm) and by 1.0 to 1.7 mm (belly, chest). The wave is zero within about 2 cm of a contact centre and fades near the hand capsules; it can never move a skin point by more than half its distance to the nearest capsule (`RippleHandClearMm`), so it cannot carry the skin into the hand.
  * **Anatomical extent** (`bRippleWaveAnatomy`, `bRippleWaveCarry`, `RippleWaveChestFullAt`; measured on the sample body, palm press 1.5 cm, 60 fps, ring-down peak after the release, local normal component, two runs each, left side; the right side of the chest, belly and buttock gave the same numbers within the run-to-run spread). The wave of a touched region is one standing-wave texture of the whole region, so it used to know nothing about the soft part that was touched: a belly press waved up to the underside of the chest (3.6 mm at z 108 to 111 against 3.7 mm next to the hand) and a buttock press stopped at the border of the region the buttock happens to be split into (5.0 mm at z 88, 1.9 mm at z 90, 0.0 mm from z 92 up to the top of the mound at z 97). Now the wave is scaled by the soft part.
    * Belly press: underbust (z 108 to 111) 3.6 to 0.5 mm, that is 0.97 to 0.14 of the 3 to 8 cm ring next to the hand and 1.46 to 0.21 of the 8 to 12 cm ring; the fade is centred at the solar plexus (the level has fallen to 50 % at z 105.5 for the belly press, the ramp is centred at z 106.6); the belly sides and flank (z 90 to 100) are unchanged (1.68 mm before and after).
    * Buttock press: mound vertices above 1 mm 70 to 86 % (centre press) and 69 to 95 % (side press); z 92 to 97 against z 84 to 88 0.00 to 0.50 (centre) and 0.00 to 0.42 (side); amplitude at the base of the mound 0.31 to 0.85 and 0.18 to 1.09 of the 8 to 12 cm ring; the wave beyond the base (membership 0.02 to 0.2) is 0.21 and 0.24 of that ring (the floor `RippleWaveOtherZoneGain` 0.1 and the fade-out); contralateral buttock 0.00 mm. Above the hand the wave used to end in a cliff (1.9, 0.4, then 0.0 mm per cm); now it falls over the whole mound (3.8, 3.0, 2.7, 2.7, 2.3, 2.1, 2.0, 1.4, 1.0, 0.9, 0.6 mm per cm).
    * Chest press: beyond the base of the chest 0.42 to 0.14 (centre) and 0.56 to 0.18 (side) of the 8 to 12 cm ring amplitude; the largest 1 cm step along the profiles 0.65 to 0.54 (centre) and 0.45 to 0.26 (side) of that amplitude; amplitude at the base up / down / lateral / medial 1.00 / 0.73 / 0.54 / 0.34 to 0.85 / 0.55 / 0.38 / 0.21 (centre press) and 0.93 to 0.88 below the breast (side press). The wave that used to run on across the belly (0.7 to 1.0 of the ring) is gone. The chest is the part this feature serves least: the base of the chest is not brought to 0.4 of the ring amplitude everywhere (medial base 0.2, lateral base 0.4) because the amplitude is the standing-wave texture of the region, which has a trough (chest, mid-mound band z 114 to 118: 1.6 to 2.1 mm against 3.1 mm at z 119 to 123) that is the shape of the baked low modes, not a seam. `RippleWaveChestFullAt` trades this: 1.0 = amplitude at the base 0.62 / 0.36 / 0.27 / 0.17 and largest step 0.40; 0.7 (default) = 0.85 / 0.55 / 0.38 / 0.21 and 0.54; 0.5 = 0.96 / 0.50 / 0.42 / 0.26 and 0.57 (and more wave beyond the base: 0.11, 0.14, 0.16 of the ring).
    * The top of the chest: up to this version the render maps left a band with a low weight sum between the front torso and the collar region (sample body, centre line, z 123 / 124 / 125 / 126 / 127: 1.00 / 0.76 / 0.31 / 0.05 / 0.88), so the wave dropped from about 1.9 to 0.4 mm over 1 cm about 3 cm below the collarbone. With the seam blend of the importer (see Region seams under [What SoftPoke computes](#what-softpoke-computes)) the sum there is 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 / 0.98 / 0.99 and the wave runs on to the top of the front torso region (chest press, profile upwards: 1.9 1.7 0.4 0.4 0.5 mm at 10 to 14 cm before, 2.2 3.2 2.9 1.0 0.8 mm now). A step remains one bin higher (2.9 to 1.0 mm), where the collar region takes over: its lower rim is fixed in the bake of the sample (displacement 0 at that rim) and it receives only the small carried wave, so the weights alone cannot continue the wave into the neck. The free rim of the front torso also waves more than its interior (3.2 mm at 11 cm against 1.8 mm at 10 cm).
    * Thigh and upper arm are unchanged (15 to 20 cm median / 90th percentile 0.70 / 1.64 and 0.56 / 1.57 mm before, 0.71 / 1.64 and 0.55 / 1.57 after, contact peak 15.24 and 13.79 mm both, residual 0.000 mm), and with the switch off the result lies within the run-to-run noise of the previous version. Side effect on the chest press: the median of the 15 to 20 cm ring rises from 1.68 to 2.00 mm and its slew (90th percentile of the change per tick) from 3.55 to 4.6 mm, and the amplitude near the root of the chest region from 0.49 to 1.14 mm (median), because the neighbouring collar and back regions now carry the wave. A 2 minute chest knead stays flat (15 to 20 cm 90th percentile 0.37 to 0.41 mm in every 24 s window, no NaN), so does a 2 minute knead on the buttock (90th percentile over the mound 1.48 to 1.52 mm in every window after the first, no NaN, 0.04 mm after the release).
    * Cost, frame GPU time on the sample body in a desktop play session, three alternating runs each: two hands on the upper arms 9.24 to 9.35 ms with the ripple off and on (+0.10 ms; the previous version +0.22 ms, the version before the anatomical extent +0.09 ms); two hands kneading the chest 5.54 to 5.66 ms without and with the anatomical extent (+0.12 ms, ripple off 5.48 ms); two hands kneading the buttocks 7.11 to 7.28 ms (+0.17 ms, ripple off 6.99 ms). The extra passes are RipWaveExport 3 us and RipCarry 6 to 9 us per region while a mound wave rings; a region that is awake but not in contact takes the idle short path plus the carrier passes, about a tenth of a full solve.
    * How it works and what it cannot do: the soft masses are found once at load time on a worker thread (convexity of a 3.5 cm neighbourhood, grid of 76 x 48 x 100 voxels of 0.75 cm = 1.4 MB, 0.3 s; the main thread spends about 1 ms to pack and upload the grid and 2 ms to pack and send the carrier maps of 12 regions, 6 MB on the GPU that the residency budget does not count). A contact is classified by its position (mound, belly, none), latched while the wave rings and classified again when the contact moves 8 cm; a change of the soft part under a held contact is cross-faded (`softpoke.RippleAnatFadeMs`, 60 ms time constant; a rub from the belly up onto the chest showed no measurable difference in the largest per-tick change of the vertices 10 cm from the palm, 6.1 to 6.9 mm with and without it, which the moving contact itself dominates). Where the touched region ends inside a mound, the regions that own the rest of it add the same wave (they must be resident: no region is loaded for it; a region that was touched itself keeps its own wave where it is as strong). A region that is touched by a second hand more than 25 cm from the other contact keeps the full wave of its own touch. Two regions that are both touched in the same mound pass each other's wave on with one tick of delay: a carried value never exceeds the larger of the own and the published one, nothing grows, and the exchange ends when one of the regions falls asleep. That second hop brings the wave to the far side of a mound that is split over three regions; `softpoke.RippleCarryExportFirst 1` publishes the own wave before the carry instead (single hop) and loses 5 to 9 points of the coverage in the back-torso part of a buttock (0.92 and 0.84 of its mound vertices above 1 mm against 0.83 and 0.79). The mound search was tuned on one body (adult sample): another character may need other thresholds, and a character without a recognised skeleton keeps the old shape (log line). A touch at the edge of a mound is scaled by the floor divided by the envelope at the contact centre (up to 6.7 times), so such a touch lets more wave through beyond the mound than `RippleWaveOtherZoneGain` says (0.61 under the breast at z 110.6, where the membership is 0.05). Right breast, right buttock and a 3 cm press were measured once and behave like the left side and the 1.5 cm press; kneading across the border between the belly and the chest and two hands on one mound were not measured except in the soaks.
  * The wave stays inside the touched region unless it is carried (`bRippleWaveCarry`, a mound that is split over regions): on a thigh or an arm it reaches the base and the far side of the limb (median 1 to 2.5 mm on the opposite skin in the press test, 0.14 to 0.5 mm at the base of the thigh, which is 40 cm from the touch), but the back of the torso is another region (`torso_back`). Pressing the belly or chest does not move the back skin (0.00 mm; the far side of the chest inside the front region moves 0.25 / 0.7 mm, median / 90th percentile), and `bRippleCoupling` only passes the small physical wave across the border. Exporting the wave across the seams is not implemented.
  * The wave follows the contact load, so a stiff, thin spot (belly: 4 mm dent for 15 mm of hand travel) kicks the region less than a soft one for the same stroke; the shape normalisation removes the dependence on the baked response of the point, not on the stroke.
  * The bulge needs the baked style data of the region (all regions of the sample body have it; a region without it simply has no bulge) and the far-field contact points (`bLimitFarField` or the bulge itself computes them).
  * Region borders (`bRippleCoupling`, off by default): borders are found at run time from the rest meshes of the regions (no re-bake needed), up to 5 neighbours per region, about 190 strip nodes per neighbour. The wave only crosses into regions that are resident (on-demand mode: a region that is not loaded stops it) and a region keeps jiggling while a neighbour is touched. That is why it is off by default: on the sample body with both hands on the torso it adds about 2.4 ms GPU (RTX 4090) because the thighs, glutes and arms next to the torso stay awake. Bent torsos use the rest frames of the regions for the transfer (approximation).
  * Face, head, neck, hands and feet use 0.4 of the strength (`RippleRegionScale`).
  * Safety: the receiving oscillators are capped (`RippleMaxMm`), a NaN guard resets them, and a region that is excited from a neighbour wakes, and a region whose own ripple motion is above the rest threshold is not skipped as at rest. Below the threshold a coupled wave may still advance the hidden receiver state inside the idle path (the amplitude read-back that decides this is 2-4 frames old, so a wave that arrives at a sleeping region becomes visible a few frames late). The effective transfer (`RippleCouplingGain` x (1 - `RippleHopLoss`)) is capped at 0.11: measured on the thigh/torso/glute neighbourhood the loop between coupled regions settles at or below 0.10 and keeps a 0.25 to 0.5 mm limit cycle from 0.16 on, so higher slider values give no more than the cap. Closed loops were only tested there (5-minute knead, no drift); treat `bRippleCoupling` as experimental. The bulge is faded near the hand capsules (`RippleHandClearMm`); it starts 0.67 x `RippleBulgeNearMm` from each contact and is capped at `RippleBulgeMaxMm` and half the tissue thickness.
* **Region seams.** A body is baked as overlapping regions. Each region's displacement fades out towards the rim of its patch and the importer blends the regions where they overlap. Where two regions overlapped by less than twice the fade length, or only touched rim to rim, the sum of the weights fell to about 0 along the seam and the skin there did not move: a finger pressed 13 to 15 mm into it left a dent of 0.0 to 1.9 mm next to 3.5 to 10.8 mm one to two centimetres away (sample body: knee, elbow, front of the hip, back of the shoulder, top of the chest). The importer now makes every seam between regions a partition of unity (**seam blend**, on by default, `bRenderMapSeamBlend` / `import_folder ... seamblend=0` for the old weights): each region is extended up to 4 cm past its free rim (the rim displacement, constant) and the regions crossfade continuously. Measured on the sample body (finger, 1 cm press, dent in mm, valley and the two sides): knee 3.3 to 9.6 (sides 6.7 / 9.8), elbow 1.3 to 9.9 (3.9 / 9.7), front of the hip 1.9 to 8.9 (9.2 / 7.5), back of the shoulder 0.5 to 3.9 (one side 7.5); at the line where the front torso meets the collar region (top of the chest) the finger now dents 3.8 mm against 9.6 / 8.0 mm 1 to 3 cm beside (the limiter lets the finger in only 8.4 mm there against 10.5 / 13.5 mm beside). The weight sum in every overlap band between two regions is now 1.00 (it was as low as 0.00 to 0.19), in the abutting bands 1.00 at the 10th percentile. Not changed: the true outer edges of the baked skin (wrists, ankles, the top of the neck, the crotch) keep their fade; skin that no region maps over more than 15 mm stays still (on the sample: a strip at the back of the neck between the back torso and the collar region, z 124 to 131, and the top of the shoulders between the collar and the upper arms); a fixed (clamped) rim of a region is not extended, so where such a rim meets another region the crossfade happens on the other region's side only. Cost: 7 % more render map entries on the sample (228,299 to 244,693, +1.2 MB of map data), +0.05 ms frame GPU time with two hands on the upper arms, +0.00 / +0.02 ms with two hands kneading the chest / buttocks, about 0.6 s more import time.
* **Release.** When the hand leaves, the tissue returns. A trailing of roughly 0.2-0.5 s on slow movements is the cost of the input filter.

## What is not simulated

* **Clothes.** Clothing that is a separate mesh over the skin does not deform with the skin. Fitted clothes that are part of the same mesh move with the skin vertices they are attached to, but have no physics of their own.
* **Anything but the hand capsules** (and the optional head collider) as contact objects: no props, no other characters, no self-contact of the body.
* **Hair, cloth, fluids, bones** and any other physics. SoftPoke is complementary to the engine's own cloth and soft-body tools.
* **Blend-shape expressions** and the jaw are not followed by the baked `face` and `neck_collar` regions: they follow the head and neck bones only (see [Face and neck](#face-and-neck)).
* **Multiplayer / replication, mobile, consoles, Mac, Linux.**
* **Fast-slap realism.** Very fast or very strong hits fall outside the small-deformation model; the displacement is clamped (`DisplacementKneeMm`, `DisplacementMaxMm`).

## Face and neck

* **Static rest face.** The `face` region is attached to the head bone and `neck_collar` to the neck bone. They dent the resting expression and move with those bones. A character that opens the mouth, smiles or turns the jaw through blend shapes or face joints does not carry the dent with the changing face shape. VRoid (VRM) expressions are blend shapes, so this is the normal case for them.
* **Keep-out zones.** Eyes, nose, lips, nostrils and ears are not part of the face region (a margin of 14 mm around them): there the surface shows no response. A poke on a closed eyelid or on a lip does nothing. The hand does not pass through these openings: the penetration limiter closes them with a shallow dome (`softpoke.LimiterCapHoles`), so the fingers stop on it. The dome is lifted onto the real surface of the mesh inside the opening (nose, eyelids, lips) and gets a thin allowed depth (about 1 mm, the opening does not deform), so a fingertip stops on the nose instead of sinking into it (`softpoke.LimiterLiftCaps`; in a cooked build the body mesh needs Allow CPU Access on LOD 0, otherwise the flat dome stays: a little inside the nose tip, a little in front of the eyes). A finger pressed head-on against such a rigid spot stops straight; the whole proxy hand moves back instead of the finger folding over it (`Limiter.AxialPushRigidBelowMm`).
* **Bony landmarks (all regions).** The baked thickness is the distance to a simplified bone inside the region and is far too large over bony prominences. With `bAnatomicalThickness` (default on) the limiter and the per-vertex clamp use adult soft-tissue depths at bony landmarks that are found on the skin from the skeleton at load time: patella 5 mm, tibia 5 mm, malleoli 4 mm, olecranon 4 mm, wrist 4.5 mm, clavicle 6 mm, sternum line 7 mm, spinous processes 6 mm, zygoma 6 mm, mandible border 8 mm, chin 10 mm, forehead 5 mm (`AnatomyTissueScale` scales them). Skin that bulges away from a bone keeps its depth (breast tissue over the sternum, the thigh next to the knee). These are textbook averages for an adult, not a measured map of your character.
* **Long bones as lines.** The shafts of the radius and ulna (forearm), the humerus (upper arm) and the tibia (lower leg) are bone lines inside the limb: the thickness of the skin is its distance from the nearest shaft. The forearm is no longer one soft tube (a deep press now stops at about 21 mm instead of 27 mm).
* **Buccal cheek.** The cheek between the cheekbone and the jaw is *thicker* than the bake (24 mm; the only place where the model adds tissue; core radius 1.3 cm, fading out by 2.8 cm), so a cheek poke gives a clearly readable soft dimple: about 14 mm at a normal press in our test (the bake alone: 8 mm), as much as a forearm poke.
* **Bone shadow.** Skin behind the bone under a contact does not move (`bBoneShadow`): the far side of the forearm and of the upper arm stays still under a deep press (before 2.1 mm and 1.4 mm).
* **Finger joints stay anatomical.** Every finger joint of the tracked hand (after the input filter), of the proxy hand (after every push-back step) and of the visible hand stays inside these ranges (`USoftPokeHandRig::JointLimits`, switch `Limiter.bAnatomicalJointLimits`; visible hand: `SoftPokeHandMeshDriver.bAnatomicalJointLimits`; all on by default):

  | Joint | Flexion (toward the palm) | Hyperextension (backward) | Sideways |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Knuckle (MCP) | 0-90 deg | 25 deg | +-20 deg |
  | Middle joint (PIP) | 0-105 deg | 5 deg | none (hinge) |
  | End joint (DIP) | 0-85 deg | 15 deg | none (hinge) |
  | Thumb knuckle / thumb end joint | bend at most 70 / 85 deg from the previous segment | | |

  The phalanges do not twist about their own axis, and bone lengths never change. The push-back of a finger blocked by the skin may only bend a joint **back** (less flexion, down to the hyperextension limit) relative to the tracked finger; it never curls it further or bends it sideways. Beyond that the finger stays straight and the whole proxy hand stops / moves back. Angles are measured about each finger's hinge axis (wrist -> knuckle x palm normal, turned with the knuckle's sideways angle), so a fully curled finger keeps the right sign. Without the limits a strong poke could fold the end of the finger sideways or backward by 20-60 deg (our audit: 341 joints outside these ranges in one test run, 0 with the limits).
* **Over bone, contact is smoothed.** Tracker jitter of a few mm is as large as the whole dent over a kneecap; the rig filters the motion along the contact normal more strongly there (`ThinTissueAllowedMm`), so a held press on the kneecap stays between 4.3 and 5.7 mm under 3 mm jitter (before 0.2-5.6 mm).
* **Estimated thickness.** The soft-tissue thickness of the face and neck is an engineering estimate per area (cheek, zygomatic arch, forehead, jaw line, clavicle band, trapezius ...), not a measured map; the numbers are plausible, not validated against anatomy. For a thin neck (stylised characters) the tool limits the thickness to 45 % of the local neck thickness.
* **Landmarks are found on the mesh.** A coarse or stylised head works, but the eye, mouth and ear positions of a head that offers no cue are estimated from proportions; the gate item `face_landmarks` says which cue was used. A head with hair, a hat or glasses in the baked mesh cannot be measured: the two regions are skipped.
* **Cost.** The two regions are among the more expensive ones: the data of a face is about 16 MB and of the neck about 20 MB on a full-resolution head (VRoid test avatar: 17 MB and 6 MB), and a poke that touches both pays both solves. They are loaded only when touched.

## Tuning

Parameters are visual and stability controls, not measured tissue values. Change one at a time, by at most a factor of two. The full property list with defaults is in the [component reference](ComponentReference.md); the Tune skill (`Extras/AgentSkills/softpoke-tune`) has the symptom-by-symptom procedure.

| Symptom | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Shimmer at fingertips while the hand is still | Tracker jitter amplified by stylize. Check `bFilter` and `bContactFilter` on the rig; raise `StylizeLowPassMs` / `StylizeLowPassHoldMs`; lower `StylizeStrength`. |
| A fingertip sinks too deep | Lower `Limiter.ThicknessFraction`; check that hand regions have `TipContact` = `Auto` or `On`. |
| Bony spots too stiff or too soft | `AnatomyTissueScale` (above 1 = softer); `bAnatomicalThickness` off = the baked thickness only. Look at `AnatomySummary` to see which landmarks were found. |
| Skin far from the hand moves slightly | `bLimitFarField`, `FarFieldRadiusMm`. |
| The far side of a limb should follow more / less | `BoneShadowStartMm` (larger = follows more), `bBoneShadow` off = the whole cross-section moves. |
| Deep presses too deep / too shallow | `ThicknessMaxFraction` (0.75: the dent stays within the limiter's allowed depth). |
| Dent invisible on a toon character | Denser display mesh, then the [dent overlay](BakeYourOwnCharacter.md#the-dent-overlay-toon-characters). |
| Too stiff or too soft | Stylize amount; for the baked response itself, a re-bake with a softer tissue preset (`--tissue-style soft`) and a higher `JiggleFreqScale`. |
| Far skin hardly moves after a push | Raise `RippleWaveGain` (up to 3, and `RippleWaveMaxMm` for more than 5 mm), `RippleWaveReachCm` and `RippleBulgeFraction`. The wave stays in the touched region; `bRippleCoupling` only passes the small physical wave to the neighbouring regions at a GPU cost. |
| The chest wave runs into the belly or the belly wave into the chest | `bRippleWaveAnatomy` (on), `RippleWaveOtherZoneGain` (0 = none outside the touched soft part), `RippleWaveZoneBlendCm` (width of the transition). |
| The wave of a buttock stops at a region border | `bRippleWaveCarry` (on) and the neighbouring regions loaded (on-demand mode); `NumRippleCarriers` shows how many regions carry the wave. |
| The wave looks too strong, too long or too jittery | Lower `RippleWaveGain` (0 = physical only), raise `RippleWaveDamping`, lower `RippleWaveCutHz` for a slower, wider wave. |
| Jiggle missing, too strong or too bouncy | `bJiggle`, `JiggleAmount`, `JiggleDamping`, `JiggleFreqScale`, `JiggleImpact`. There are no shipped jiggle presets yet; start from "calm" (`JiggleAmount` 0.6, `JiggleDamping` 1.5) or "default". |
| Sluggish hand | Filter settings of the rig (`MinCutoffHz` up, or `Beta` up). |

## Performance

The cost depends on how many regions are awake near a hand; regions far from the hand cost nothing and hold no solver state. Only an RTX 4090 has been measured; **other GPUs are not validated** (AMD / Intel: not tested, expected to work on DX12 SM6 GPUs). In a cooked Development build with two regions in contact, the solve took on average 3.3-3.5 ms per frame (maximum 4.9-6.8 ms). Heavier cases cost more: both hands pressing the upper arms of the sample wake 7 regions and take about 4.8 ms on an RTX 4090, and while the engine renders in stereo the solves are kept within `SolveGpuBudgetMs` (automatic 3 ms), see [VR](VR.md#performance).

Read the timings live from the component: `LastGpuMs`, `AvgGpuMs`, `MaxGpuMs`, `GpuTimeByActiveCount`, `NumActiveRegions`, `ResidentMB`, `NumLateContactFrames`; the log prints "SoftPoke GPU ... ms" every `GpuTimingLogInterval` solves; `stat GPU` also works. An unfocused editor window is throttled by the engine and inflates the numbers: measure with the viewport focused or in a packaged game.

Both hands pressing the body wake more regions than one finger on a cheek, because every region within range of a hand stays awake before it has contact. An awake region that the hands provably cannot change in this frame (no skin node within the contact band, nothing for the stylize pass to lift or push, no jiggle motion, and an earlier solve that read back an empty contact set and a zero result) takes a short path: only its approach-side latch and stylize low-pass state are updated, exactly as the full solve would, and it adds the same zero displacement. In the sample, both hands pressing the upper arms wake 7 regions; 3 of them take the short path and the solver takes about 4.8 ms of GPU time on an RTX 4090 instead of 6.4 ms (one region on the cheek: about 0.7 ms), plus about 10 ms on the render thread in desktop PIE. This is the case to check against a VR frame budget. `softpoke.IdleSkip 0` turns the short path off, `2` keeps the full solves and counts any that the short path would have replaced but that read back a non-zero result (`NumIdleVerifyMismatches` on the component, expected 0); `NumIdleSkippedSolves` counts the short paths.

Game thread: the hand rig's penetration limiter is the main cost. It runs on worker threads when there is enough work (`softpoke.LimiterParallelMinPairs`; the result does not depend on it). With both hands in contact the plugin took about 1.5 ms (cheeks) to 3 ms (both hands pressing the upper body) per frame on a Ryzen 7 7800X3D in desktop PIE with the sample standing in its rest pose; an animated character can cost more, because fewer hand samples can be rejected before the anchor search. To read it in your project: `csvprofile start`, `csvprofile stop`, then the `SoftPoke/...` columns of the CSV in `Saved/Profiling/CSV` (inclusive ms per frame: `RigTick` hand rig, `CompTick` solver preparation, `DriverTick` visible hand).

Frame budget (VR): every region in contact costs about 0.8 to 1.4 ms of GPU time on an RTX 4090 (stylize about 40 % of it), so two hands on the flanks or the hips (5 to 8 regions) cost 4 to 6 ms, more than a 75 Hz headset frame has left next to the stereo scene. `SolveGpuBudgetMs` (automatic: 3 ms while rendering in stereo) defers the solves that do not fit by one frame; such a region shows its last result for that frame and is solved first in the next one. Measured on the sample (two hands kneading a tour of chest, flanks, buttocks, outer thighs, belly and hips, 75 fps cap, 7.2 MP render, desktop PIE): frames over 13.3 ms of GPU time 207 of 5524 -> 0 of 5533, GPU frame time average 10.37 -> 8.77 ms, 99th percentile 14.27 -> 11.15 ms. With the budget a held press gives the same dent (largest difference of the time-averaged displacement 0.37 mm on a 12.5 mm chest press; on a 3 cm press into the upper arms up to 3.5 mm at the deepest point, where two runs without the budget differ by up to 2.2 mm). `softpoke.SolveBudgetMs` overrides it for tests; `LastFrameSolveGpuMs`, `NumDeferredSolves` and the `csvprofile` columns `SoftPoke/SolveFrameGpuMs`, `SoftPoke/SolvesDeferred` show it working.

Levers, cheapest first: `StylizeStrength` 0 (removes the stylize passes), `bJiggle` off, `bDentOverlay` off, fewer baked regions, a lower `JiggleModes` / `JiggleModesIdle`, `SolverRateHz` > 0 to cap the solve rate, and `VramBudgetMB` plus the streaming settings if `NumDegradedContact` is above 0.

## Platform limits

Unreal Engine 5.8, Windows 64, DirectX 12 with Shader Model 6. NVIDIA RTX validated; AMD / Intel: not tested (expected to work on DX12 SM6 GPUs, not validated). With DirectX 11 / SM5 the solver is off (one warning, no crash). The solver is created in Play-In-Editor, Simulate and game worlds, not in the plain editor viewport unless `bSolveInEditor` is on.

## OpenXR hand input (experimental, bare hands not tested on a headset)

`SoftPokeHandSourceOpenXR` reads the runtime through the engine's `IHandTracker` and, for validity and size, `IXRTrackingSystem::GetHandTrackingState` (UE 5.8; the older `DeviceVisualType == Hand` check of `FXRMotionControllerData` does not exist in 5.8 - this API only describes hands).

* **Validity:** a hand is used only when the runtime says it is tracked (`IHandTracker` flag) and the tracking system's hand state is valid (`FXRHandTrackingState::bValid`, `TrackingStatus == Tracked`). `bAcceptUntracked` skips both checks (last known joints).
* **Per joint:** the engine gives one validity flag for the whole hand, not one per joint. SoftPoke drops the whole frame when any of the 26 joints has a non-finite position or a zero rotation, so a half-valid hand never reaches the limiter or the visible mesh.
* **Size:** when the runtime reports joint radii (`HandKeyRadii`), the mean radius of the finger joints divided by `ReferenceJointRadiusCm` (default 0.75 cm; set 0 to ignore radii) is the hand's size ratio (`FSoftPokeHandFrame::HandSizeRatio`, rig status `SourceHandSizeRatio`). The visible hand multiplies its fitted size by that ratio, **clamped to 0.85 .. 1.15**, as one uniform scale. Without radii (ratio 0, as for UDP and the built-in tracker) the size fitted from the joint distances is used alone. The reference radius is a typical value, not measured on a headset: if your runtime reports larger or smaller radii the clamp keeps the hand within 15 percent of the fitted size, and `ReferenceJointRadiusCm` is the knob.

## Motion controllers (synthesized hand)

`SoftPokeHandSourceController` (also used by `Auto` when no OpenXR hand is tracked) builds a hand from the grip pose of a controller and its trigger, grip and thumb-touch values. Checked with Valve Index controllers on one headset setup (see [VR](VR.md)); other controllers are not verified.

* **What it is:** an average adult hand (wrist to middle knuckle 8.6 cm, scaled by `HandScale`) in procedural poses: relaxed, fist, and the pointing hand (trigger released with the grip held). Each finger moves between "relaxed" (MCP / PIP / DIP about 9 / 12 / 8 degrees) and "fist" (85 / 100 / 60) with the input; the thumb moves between open and tucked across the palm. Poses are never taken from a recording, so there is no sideways finger motion apart from a small fan of the relaxed fingers.
* **Joint limits:** the synthesized hand is inside the default anatomical `JointLimits` over the whole input range (a test over 250 input combinations for each hand: at most 1 mm of correction by the clamp). The rig applies the limits to its target again like for any tracked hand.
* **Contact:** from the rig on nothing differs from a tracked hand: filter bypass aside, the penetration limiter, the allowed depth law, the per-finger relaxation and the joint limits of the proxy hand all apply. A fist or a grip on a limb is limited like a tracked fist. Squeezing the grip curls the fingers by the input, not by a force: pressing deeper means moving the controller deeper (the hand stops at the allowed depth, the real controller does not).
* **No finger tracking:** fingers that are not on the trigger, grip or thumb sensors are not known. Typing, pinching and independent finger motion are not reproduced; middle, ring and little finger always close together. Valve Index controllers have finger sensors: with SteamVR OpenXR hand tracking `Auto` uses those joints instead (this path is not tested).
* **Placement:** the palm centre of the virtual hand is at the grip pose origin. The grip pose is defined by the OpenXR runtime per controller; `ProfileTweaks` / `GlobalTweak` correct the remaining offset. Defaults are zero: expect to need a few millimetres to centimetres of adjustment on your headset.
* **Latency:** the controller source skips the rig's webcam filter, so the hand follows the controller pose directly; the fingers follow the inputs at `FingerCurlSpeed` (default 8 curl units per second, about 0.12 s from open to closed).
* **Haptics:** a short weak pulse at contact start and at clearly deeper presses (default on, amplitude at most 0.35). Whether the runtime plays it is not tested.

## Content rules

SoftPoke is for **adult humanoid characters**. The bake tool's quality gate rejects child-like proportions and the support does not cover them. Do not use SoftPoke to create content that the Fab content rules or the law forbid.
