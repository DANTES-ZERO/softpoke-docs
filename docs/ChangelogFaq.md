# Changelog, known issues, FAQ

## Changelog

### 1.0.0 - initial release

* **Low-VRAM profile for the demo in VR:** with a headset the demo game mode picks an 8 GB or a 6 GB render profile from the video memory of the GPU (`softpoke.Demo.VRLowVram` overrides it). Packaged demo, peak of a tour, game process: 5.6 GB with the profile off (3.6 MP per eye), 2.7 GB with the 8 GB profile (3.6 MP per eye), 2.3 GB with the 6 GB profile (2.5 MP per eye); the SteamVR compositor needs about 2.6 GB more. Checked by eye on one headset; not checked on a real 8 GB or 6 GB graphics card. DLSS is optional and not shipped. See [VR](VR.md#low-vram--quest-3-pc-vr).
* **Esc quits** a packaged or standalone run of the demo maps (`bEscapeQuitsGame` on the demo game mode, default on; the editor and your own game modes are not affected).
* **Smaller VRAM footprint:** the resident region data is packed losslessly (near-field codes stored as 4-bit values with a shared scale table and no separate row index, 8.5 instead of 21 bytes per block). With every region resident the sample body needs 340 MB instead of 561 MB, regions become ready sooner (upload and init up to 8x faster per region), and the contact solve is slightly faster (RTX 4090, one region: 0.73 -> 0.69 ms on the back of the hand, 1.00 -> 0.94 ms on the thigh). The results are bit-identical to before.
* **VR frame budget and residency:** while kneading close to the body with both hands the headset view faded for a moment. Cause: the region solves cost 4 to 7 ms of GPU time with 5 to 9 regions in contact (single frames up to 18 ms), and with the 400 MB VRAM budget the sample body (561 MB at that time) kept evicting and reloading regions (22 evictions, uploads of 10 to 100 MB that added 1.5 to 7 ms to their frames). New `SolveGpuBudgetMs` (automatic: 3 ms while rendering in stereo): solves that do not fit wait one frame, show their last result and are solved first in the next one with the skipped time added. `VramBudgetMB` is now 640 MB. Two hands kneading a tour of chest, flanks, buttocks, thighs, belly and hips (75 fps cap, 7.2 MP render, RTX 4090): frames over 13.3 ms of GPU time 207 -> 0, GPU frame average 10.37 -> 8.77 ms, evictions 11 -> 0, loads 18 -> 10 (each region once). New read-only `LastFrameSolveGpuMs`, `MaxFrameSolveGpuMs`, `NumDeferredSolves`, `TotalDeferredSolves`, `LastPredictedSolveMs`, csvprofile columns `SoftPoke/SolveFrameGpuMs`, `SolvesDeferred`, `SolvePredictedMs`, `SolvePlans`, console variable `softpoke.SolveBudgetMs`.
* **Chest wave over the collarbone:** a chest press left a step in the wave towards the collarbone (2.9 -> 1.0 mm within 1 cm at the top of the chest, with a bump on the chest side of it). Cause: next to the region rim the baked mode shapes barely move, the shape normalisation of the wave multiplied their noise up to the soft limit (the bump), the collar region showed little of it, and the mound membership of the chest fades out within about 1 cm there. New `RippleWaveShapeGainMax` (6) / `RippleWaveShapeGainExponent` (0.5) limit that gain, `RippleWaveSeamBandCm` (4) lets the rim band of the touched region and its neighbours show the wave of its interior, `RippleWaveChestFadeCm` (6) lets the chest fade out over 6 cm (the buttocks keep 4), `RippleWaveMoundBlurCm` exposes the membership smoothing (1). No re-bake. Palm press on the chest, profile upwards (median of the normal ring-down per 1 cm): largest step between 8 and 16 cm 65 % -> 12 %, the wave now runs on to about 19 cm (over the collarbone) and fades there; belly, side of the chest, under the chest and buttock profiles unchanged within run-to-run noise (largest 8-16 cm step changes -0.14 to +0.06).
* Region seams: the importer makes every seam between two baked regions a partition of unity (seam blend: each region extended up to 4 cm past its free rim, continuous crossfade; true outer edges keep their fade). Before, the weight sum fell to about 0 where two regions overlapped narrowly or touched rim to rim, and a finger pressed into such a seam left almost no dent (sample body, 1 cm press: knee 3.3 / elbow 1.3 / front of the hip 1.9 mm against 6.7 to 10.8 mm beside; now 9.6 / 9.9 / 8.9 mm). The top of the chest no longer cuts the contact ripple 3 cm below the collarbone. The shipped sample was re-imported (no re-bake; render maps and skin fill changed, asset names unchanged). Switch: Bake Tool setting `bRenderMapSeamBlend`, `import_folder ... seamblend=0` (also `seamreach=`, `seamgap=`). Cost on the sample: +7 % render map entries, +0.05 ms frame GPU (two hands on the upper arms). See [Behaviour and limits](BehaviourAndLimits.md#what-softpoke-computes) (Region seams).
* GPU contact solver for baked regions: poke, grab, squeeze, friction; stylize ("mochi") layer; jiggle layer; bend following; on-demand region loading.
* Built-in webcam hand tracker `SoftPokeHandSourceNative` (no Python: Media Foundation capture, MediaPipe hand models through NNE / NNERuntimeORT, CPU or DirectML; follow and recenter through `SoftPokeTrackerLink`). Tested with a virtual camera (EpocCam) and recorded hand takes; not yet tested with a physical webcam in a cooked build. It runs on the engine's experimental NNE / NNERuntimeORT plugin.
* Hand input: UDP (webcam tracker, protocol v1 with head fields), OpenXR hand tracking (experimental), **VR controllers** (experimental; `SoftPokeHandSourceController` synthesizes the hand from the grip pose and trigger / grip / thumb-touch values with shipped Enhanced Input assets for Valve Index, Oculus Touch, Vive and WMR, haptics on contact; `SoftPokeHandSourceAuto` = OpenXR hands, else controllers, else the webcam; Valve Index controllers through SteamVR OpenXR hand tracking use the existing OpenXR path), penetration limiter ("physics hand"), optional head collider, face driver (ARKit-named curves). The default `Source Class` of the rig is now `SoftPokeHandSourceAuto` (it was the OpenXR source; with nothing tracked it behaves the same).
* Game-thread cost of the hand rig with both hands in contact reduced (limiter query, capsule preparation; the contact result is unchanged): nearest-node search with subtree boxes, anchor search through group spheres, hand samples rejected before the anchor search, work on worker threads (`softpoke.LimiterParallelMinPairs`). `csvprofile` columns `SoftPoke/...`, see [Performance](BehaviourAndLimits.md#performance).
* VR fixes from headset use: controller haptics are re-issued every frame for the pulse length and also sent to the controller while OpenXR hand tracking is the active source (Valve Index); the demo game mode caps the headset render resolution (`MaxVRMegapixelsPerEye`, default 3.6 MP per eye, CVar `softpoke.Demo.VRMaxMegapixelsPerEye`) so a high supersampling setting cannot stall the GPU.
* **Contact ripple:** a push, release or knead now makes the touched region wave beyond the hand's reach (the dynamic part of the jiggle layer is exempt from the far-field limit), a wide soft bulge rises around a dent (volume bounded by the displayed dent volume, low-passed, returns to rest with the load) and, as an opt-in (`bRippleCoupling`), the wave crosses region borders (borders are found at run time, no re-bake). New properties in the category `SoftPoke|Ripple`, console variable `softpoke.Ripple`. The dent next to the hand and the contact result are unchanged; with `bRipple` off the output is that of the previous build (a few small solver buffers are larger and the render passes bind the style buffers, measured cost difference below 0.05 ms).
 Contact ripple 2: a new display-only wave (`bRippleWave`, `RippleWaveGain`, `RippleWaveReachCm`, `RippleWaveDamping`, `RippleWaveCutHz`, `RippleWaveStrokeMs`, `RippleWaveMaxMm`) kicks the low modes of the touched region by the rate of the contact load; the wave field is normalised so that it has the same size everywhere in the region and dies off exponentially beyond 8 cm. On the sample body a 1.5 cm palm press gives 0.5 to 1.9 mm (median) and 1.6 to 3.7 mm (90th percentile) at 15 to 20 cm along the skin, where the physical wave is 0.01 mm; it shows as a moving shading and silhouette in a video (1 to 3 pixels at 720p), not in a still image, rings for one to three cycles and is at rest after about 1 s. New `RippleHandClearMm` fades the wave and the bulge near the hand capsules and limits their motion to half the distance to the nearest capsule, `RippleBulgeFraction` goes up to 8 (default 3, was 0.3) and `RippleBulgeMaxMm` up to 8 (default 3), the bulge ring defaults are 40 / 80 mm. The wave is display only: the depth of the dent at the touch is unchanged (peak within 0.02 mm at the defaults), the cost with the ripple on is about +0.1 to +0.2 ms GPU in the two-hand body scene (RTX 4090), and `bRipple` off still restores the previous output.
* Contact ripple 3: the wave follows the touched soft part (`bRippleWaveAnatomy`, `RippleWaveOtherZoneGain`, `RippleWaveZoneBlendCm`, `RippleWaveChestFullAt`, `bRippleWaveCarry`). The chest, the left and right buttock (convexity of the rest nodes) and the chest / abdomen ramp (skeleton heights) are found once at load time without a bake change; a contact is classified by its position and the wave is scaled by 1 inside that soft part and about 0.1 outside; the regions that own the rest of a split mound carry the wave of the touched region while it rings. Sample body, palm press 1.5 cm: belly press, underbust 3.6 to 0.5 mm (0.97 to 0.14 of the wave next to the hand); buttock press, mound vertices above 1 mm 70 to 86 % (centre) and 69 to 95 % (side), the upper part of the mound (z 92 to 97) 0.00 to 0.50 of the lower part; chest press, the wave beyond the base of the chest 0.42 to 0.14 of the 8 to 12 cm ring; thigh and upper arm unchanged (0.71 / 1.64 and 0.55 / 1.57 mm at 15 to 20 cm), contact peak, residual and 2 minute soaks unchanged, +0.10 ms frame GPU on the two-hand upper arm scene, +0.12 ms (chest) and +0.17 ms (buttocks) with two hands kneading. Limits: the standing-wave texture inside a mound stays (the base of the chest reaches 0.2 to 0.9 of the ring amplitude, the buttock 0.4 to 1.1), the collar region takes the wave over at the top of the chest with a smaller amplitude (its lower rim is fixed in the sample bake; the low weight-sum band below it is closed by the seam blend), carrying needs the neighbour regions resident, the mound search was tuned on one body.
* GPU cost of awake regions the hands do not touch reduced: such a region takes a short path when its output provably stays zero (no skin node within the contact band, no stylize lift or push-out, no jiggle motion, and a read back zero result); the approach-side latch and the stylize low-pass state are still updated as by the full solve (`softpoke.IdleSkip`, verify mode `2`). Two-hand press on the sample's upper arms: 6.4 to 4.8 ms of solver GPU time on an RTX 4090. On-demand region loading no longer reads the payload data on the game thread (the first contact after play started could hitch by about 30 ms in the editor).
* Bake panel (Window > SoftPoke Bake) with quality gate and automatic repair, bone-map detection for UE5 / MetaHuman, Mixamo / Rigify-style and VRM (`J_Bip`) skeletons, the **Set up** button for the bake Python, scriptable API for AI agents.

* Smoothed render map (importer options `smooth=` 1.8 and `smoothface=` 2.5 node spacings): rounder dent outlines on toon shading at a small cost in peak dent depth; `smooth=0` gives the earlier barycentric map. Hand and finger regions are not smoothed by default (`smoothhand=`, finely meshed, small dents, the kernel is expensive there), and a smoothed vertex reads at most 32 display nodes (`smoothcap=`).
* Dent overlay: per-slot feather overrides (`DentOverlaySlotOverrides`: slot name to `Feather`, `FeatherHiMm`); the helper script warns when it finds an older overlay material without the `Feather` parameter, and `--rebuild` (`py "<Project>/Plugins/SoftPoke/Tools/ue_build_toon_dent_materials.py" --rebuild`) rebuilds that material in place, instances and assignments kept.
* **Visible hand on the contact-resolved hand:** `SoftPokeHandMeshDriver` poses a hand skeletal mesh (your own, or the VR template hands) from the contact-resolved hand, bone by bone in the parent frame (forward kinematics with an axial bone scale, see "Head-on poke and visible hand" below), so the visible fingers follow the hand the solver uses instead of the raw tracked hand. It warns when the incoming hand looks mirrored.
* **Hand-to-hair collision (optional):** `SoftPokeHairCollision` lets the contact-resolved hand push Groom strand hair (runtime Physics Asset of finger and palm capsules, optional head / neck / shoulder colliders), and can manage the hair LOD for VR (strands only while a hand is near the head, cards otherwise). No dependency on the HairStrands plugin. See [Hair comb](HairComb.md) for the set-up and the recommended project settings.
* **Demo VR pawn:** `SoftPokeDemoVRPawn` / `BP_SoftPokeDemoVRPawn` (floor-level tracking origin, HMD camera, two motion controllers, thumbstick walking, snap turn) and `SoftPokeDemoGameMode`, which picks it in both demo maps when a headset is enabled and the desktop pawn otherwise (`softpoke.Demo.VR` overrides). See [VR](VR.md#demo-vr-pawn).
* **Desktop viewpoint and tracker link (optional):** `SoftPokeTrackerLink` sends the camera pose to the webcam tracker so the tracked hands follow your view, recenters the hand, toggles the debug drawing and the tracker preview, and can start and stop the tracker with the game. `SoftPokeDesktopFly` is a keyboard / mouse free-fly viewpoint for testing without a headset. Both are off by default.
* **Webcam tracker:** video-file input with `--start` / `--end` trimming, landmark recording and frame-locked replay (`--record-landmarks`, `--record-dir`, `replay_landmarks.py`), follow and recenter over a UDP control port (`--follow-port`), Esc Esc to quit the preview.
* **Hand, bone and demo-map fixes:** the visible hand's forearm bone follows the wrist (it was left behind and stretched the forearm) and an input guard limits implausible keypoints; the hand is hidden while not tracked (an idle hand no longer sits on the character's own hand); bone shadow (`bBoneShadow`): the far side of a limb stays still; long-bone shafts (radius, ulna, humerus, tibia) in the anatomical model; softer cheek (buccal area); smoothing over bone (`ThinTissue*`); the dent stays within the limiter's allowed depth (`ThicknessMaxFraction` 0.75); about 4x less CPU while touching the face (fps 25 -> 107 in our test); the demo maps bring their own exposure, lights, floor and no sun disc, so they look the same in any project.
* **Grab look, cheek and sample hand:** a deep grab no longer shows faceted patches and a hard crease ring (the stylize push-out is smoothed, `StylizeResolveSmoothIterations`; rounder, lower rim: `StylizeRound` 0.9, `StylizeRimHeightMm` 4); softer cheek (buccal tissue 24 mm); the sample's visible hands use the character's own skin textures with a generated knuckle / crease normal map and fade out along the forearm (no plain tube, no visible cut); natural copper hair in any project (darker base colour, less back-light, weaker hair fill light); no dark spot above the sample's left eyebrow (two backwards-wound triangles of the brow card turned the recomputed skin normals inward there; the head material now mirrors such a normal back out).

The forearm is no longer cut: the sample material folds it into a short rounded end (closed from every direction, opaque, clean in forward + MSAA and without the grainy dither).
  * The visible hand turns see-through (35 %, the fingertip in contact stays more opaque) while it is close to the camera or comes at the contact from the camera's side, so you can see where you touch: `VisibilityMode` (opaque / auto-fade / ghost, key **H** in the demo maps), `bOcclusionFade`, `FadeStartDistanceCm`, `MinOpacity`, `ContactFingerOpacity`, `OcclusionFadeMaterial`.

A final check holds the last good pose if a pose ever fails; the mesh is sized by the palm and finger lengths. `PoseReport` shows the values.

A finger blocked by the skin may only bend back a little; then the whole hand stops. A strong poke no longer shows a finger bent sideways (`Limiter.bAnatomicalJointLimits`, `JointLimits`, `SoftPokeHandMeshDriver.bAnatomicalJointLimits`).

* **Anatomically plausible touch:** bony landmarks (kneecap, shin, ankle, elbow, wrist, collarbone, cheekbone, jaw line ...) are found on the skin from the skeleton at load time and cap the tissue thickness of the penetration limiter and of a per-vertex displacement clamp, so the skin over bone barely moves while soft tissue next to it still gives (`bAnatomicalThickness`, `bPerVertexThicknessClamp`, `AnatomyTissueScale`). No rebake. Openings of a patch (the face's eye / nose / mouth opening) are closed for the limiter, so a hand cannot pass through them. Far-field motion of a region fades out beyond `FarFieldRadiusMm` from the contact (`bLimitFarField`). The proxy hand relaxes finger by finger (`Limiter.bPerFingerRelax`) and caps finger offsets (`Limiter.MaxFingerOffsetMm`), so a held press keeps its dent under tracker jitter.

## Known issues

* **VR is experimental** ([VR](VR.md)). The controller path and the demo VR pawn were run on one headset setup only: Pimax 8KX at 75 Hz, SteamVR as the OpenXR runtime, Valve Index controllers, an RTX 4090, a packaged Development build of `L_DemoPoke`. Not tested: OpenXR hand tracking without controllers (bare hands), other headsets and runtimes, a Quest 3 through Link or Virtual Desktop, a graphics card with 8 GB or 6 GB of video memory, and, on a headset, the stick key names, start height, walking speed and snap-turn feel. The controller hand is synthesized (no finger tracking) and its grip-to-palm offsets are uncalibrated zeros.
* **GPU support:** validated on NVIDIA RTX only. AMD / Intel: not tested (expected to work on DX12 SM6 GPUs, not validated). Earlier versions of the solver caused GPU driver resets under heavy buffer reuse; that cause was fixed, but only one GPU vendor has been soak-tested.
* **DirectX 11 / Shader Model 5** is not supported: the solver switches off with a warning.
* **The built-in webcam tracker runs on an experimental engine plugin.** It uses NNE / NNERuntimeORT, which Epic marks experimental / beta in UE 5.8. It was tested with a virtual camera (EpocCam) and recorded hand takes, not yet with a physical webcam in a cooked build ([Built-in webcam tracker](NativeTracking.md#status-of-testing)).
* **MetaHuman helper not yet tried by the publisher on a default MetaHuman.** `SoftPoke.Cmd prepare_metahuman` is described in the [Quick Start](QuickStart.md) (Part B); if it reports a problem, set the same things by hand.
* **Webcam depth is approximate** (MediaPipe's depth is flattened, so hand orientation is less accurate than position). Treat the webcam as casual interaction, not precision.
* **Slow movements trail** by about 0.2-0.5 s (input-filter lag).
* **Fingertips on thin, strongly curved geometry** (typical for stylised VRoid hands) can be poked less reliably than flat areas.
* **Coarse meshes** (about 18 mm edges) show dents on few vertices; use the subdivided display mesh.
* **Expression blend shapes** are not followed by face-region deformation.
* **Bony landmarks are textbook adult averages**, found from the skeleton (UE5 / MetaHuman, VRoid, Mixamo bone names). A skeleton with other names keeps the baked thickness (`AnatomySummary` on the component says so). Changing a property of `SoftPokeComponent` in the Details panel during Play re-creates its regions; a hand that is inside the skin at that moment is pushed out and comes back within about a second.
* **Clothes** that are separate meshes do not deform with the skin.
* **Hand mesh driver, palm turned sideways (supination):** with flattened webcam depth the fingers of the posed mesh can look slightly bent the wrong way. The input guard (`bGuardInput`) limits every finger bend to `MaxJointBendDeg`; the mirrored-input warning in `Status` still appears for some inputs of this kind. The deformation itself is not affected (it uses the proxy hand, not the mesh).
* **Visible hand seen along the forearm:** when the hand comes straight at the character from the camera, the camera looks at the back of the wrist. In *auto-fade* (default) the sample hand turns see-through and its wrist stub (the short forearm that carries it) fades to nothing while the forearm points at the camera, so no stump is drawn. With **H** set to *opaque* the stub stays: it ends in a rounded, tapered dome about 4.5 cm behind the wrist bone (a short blunt wrist, not a flat disc; it is still a stub, and seen end-on it fills a good part of a close view), and the back of the hand (seen from 15-20 cm it fills a 40-degree view) hides the fingers in that view. The mesh keeps its own finger proportions within 0.8-1.25 per bone: a tracked hand with very different proportions keeps a few millimetres between the visible and the solver fingertip.
* **See-through hand:** the faded hand is translucent and shows only its front-most layer: an invisible opaque copy of the hand writes the custom depth and the translucent hand drops every pixel behind it (no fingers behind the palm). This needs the project's **Custom Depth-Stencil Pass** setting to be *Enabled* (the default; with *Disabled* the hand shows all its layers again). The contact finger is tinted, the hand's edge has a subtle darker outline. A hand closer than the near plane, or a second primitive in the custom depth with a nearer surface, can still hide parts.
* **Hole caps of the face in a cooked build:** the exact stop on the nose and eyelids reads the body mesh's LOD 0 positions; without **Allow CPU Access** on that LOD the limiter keeps a flat dome over the opening (a fingertip on the nose tip can sink in up to about a centimetre). The sample mesh has it on.
* **The visible hand is clipped by the camera's near plane** when it is closer than about 10 cm to the view (an engine setting, `r.SetNearClipPlane`): what you see is the cut through the hand, not a torn mesh. Keep the hand in front of you, not at your eyes.
* **Sample visible hand, deep grab:** when the tracked hand goes far into a limb, the visible fingertips can sink a little into the skin (the proxy hand stops at the allowed depth, the skin dent is smoother and shallower than a finger). The dent itself stays smooth.
* **Sample character, face details:** a tiny skin-coloured spike at the upper edge of the left eyebrow (two backwards-wound triangles in the brow card) and a few detached lower-lash clumps under the left eye are part of the sample mesh; they are visible only from very close. A few long single hairs stand out at the temples of the sample groom.
* **Sample hair, combing:** the strands part and fall back; with fast strokes some single strands still stand out for a moment. Under the fingers the strands can fray into short bristles during a stroke (cosmetic; they recover fully after release). Stiffer, softer, smoother or thicker-collision settings of the groom were tried during development and made it worse, so the sample keeps its values.
* **Sample character: skin tone step at the wrists.** The hand, wrist and arm of the sample use separate textures; a slight colour step is visible at the wrist from close up. It is in the texture, not in the deformation.
* **Keyboard in Play-In-Editor:** the desktop view (W A S D, Q / E) and the tracker keys (C, G, T) are read from the game viewport, so they work only while the game view has keyboard focus. Click once into the game view; if keys still do nothing, make the editor the active window first (another window, e.g. a firewall prompt, can keep the focus). Shift+F1 frees the mouse again.
* **Hair LOD for VR:** the switch between strands and cards pops (UE 5.8 has no dithered transition for Grooms), and the cards are slightly paler than the strands. With Instanced Stereo on, strand hair under TSR was drawn in the wrong place in `-emulatestereo` (not checked on a headset); forward shading with MSAA made the sample hair much darker, so it is not recommended ([Rendering settings](InstallRequirements.md#rendering-settings)).
* **VR was verified on one headset setup only** (see above). The hair and hand-mesh features were checked on the desktop and in `-emulatestereo`; strand hair under TSR with Instanced Stereo was not checked on a headset.
* **Packaged game log: six `LogD3D12RHI: Error: Failed to create pipeline state ... error 80070057` lines at startup** appear in a project that has **Instanced Stereo** (`vr.InstancedStereo=True`, the XR / VR template default) enabled and runs on desktop DirectX 12. They come from the engine, not from SoftPoke: an empty cooked project with the same setting logs the same six lines, and with the `vr.*` settings removed it logs none; they are logged before the level loads and rendering is not affected. If you do not target VR, turn Instanced Stereo off in **Project Settings > Rendering > VR**.
* **A packaged game window** can be throttled by the engine in the background (an engine setting, not SoftPoke).
* **The Python webcam tracker (advanced, optional)** is included as source in `Tools/Webcam` and needs a one-time Python set-up ([Webcam tracker guide](WebcamTracker.md)). The built-in tracker ([Built-in webcam tracker](NativeTracking.md)) is the normal path and needs no Python.
* **Bake Python:** only Python 3.12 (64-bit) is supported by **Set up**; the package list is pinned by version but not by hash.
* **Export and import in the Bake panel run on the editor's main thread:** very large meshes can freeze the editor for a short time.

## FAQ

**Does it work with VR controllers (Valve Index, Quest Touch, Vive, WMR)?** Yes. Valve Index controllers were checked on one headset setup (see [VR](VR.md)); which hand source `Auto` chose during that check (the SteamVR finger-curl skeleton or the synthesized controller hand) was not recorded, so read the rig's `Status` text to see what you get. Quest Touch, Vive and WMR controllers use the same code but were not tried on a headset. With the default `Source Class` (`SoftPokeHandSourceAuto`) a hand uses OpenXR hand tracking if the runtime reports it (Valve Index controllers through SteamVR: finger-curl skeleton), else the controller: trigger curls the index finger, grip the other three, thumb touch the thumb, trigger released with the grip held points the index. See [VR](VR.md#motion-controllers) and the checklist there.

**Does it work with Chaos Flesh or Chaos Cloth?** They are complementary tools and are not combined by SoftPoke. SoftPoke handles hand-to-skin contact for baked regions; it does not simulate cloth.

**Can I use my own character?** Yes, but only after a bake: the plugin cannot deform a character that has not been baked. The bake needs 64-bit Python 3.12 and a one-time download of about 1.5 GB (plus about 2 GB for the optional GPU packages); one limb in *Draft* quality takes about 10 minutes and a whole body up to several hours. The skin must be a separate, watertight mesh without clothes, hair, eyes or teeth: a game character with its clothes built into one mesh needs its skin separated in a 3D tool such as Blender first. The baked data of the sample character fits the sample mesh only; copying the sample actor into another level is not documented or tested (use **File > Save Current Level As** to make your own copy of a demo map). Adult humanoid characters only. See [Bake Your Own Character](BakeYourOwnCharacter.md).

**Do I need a webcam, or Python?** No Python is needed for the webcam: the tracker is built in. You need a webcam (or a video file of your hand), or OpenXR hand tracking (experimental), or hand data from your own code ([API and extension](ApiAndExtension.md)). Python 3.12 is needed only for baking your own character.

**Do I need Visual Studio?** To use the plugin in the editor after a Fab / Epic Games Launcher install: it depends on what the launcher delivers. Fab builds code plugins for each engine version, but whether a built copy or the source reaches your PC was not verified by the publisher; if the editor reports missing modules or asks to compile the plugin, yes. To package a game: yes, always, also for a Blueprint-only project (a compiler that UE 5.8 accepts, see [Build tools for packaging](InstallRequirements.md#build-tools-for-packaging)). You also need it if you copy the plugin source into your project's `Plugins` folder ([Install](InstallRequirements.md#install)).

**Where is the hand mesh?** The sample content has two natural hand meshes, `SKM_NatHand2_R` / `_L` (`SoftPokeSample/Character/HandNatural`), shown in both demo maps and driven by `SoftPokeHandMeshDriver`; you may use them in your own project ([Hair comb](HairComb.md#hand-meshes)). You can also use your own hand mesh or the VR Template hands (Epic content, not shipped) on a PoseableMeshComponent ([VR](VR.md#showing-a-hand)).

**The bake is slow.** One limb in *Draft* quality takes about 10 minutes; a CPU bake of one region in *Standard* quality takes 15-20 minutes, and a whole body in *Standard* quality takes up to several hours. Use *Draft* and a short region list for tests, and the optional GPU path when the editor is closed. Close other heavy programs.

**Is a custom bake of my model offered?** No. Support covers the unmodified plugin and tools.

**Does it ship as a packaged game feature?** Yes: cook with DirectX 12 (SM6). See [Install and Requirements](InstallRequirements.md#packaging-a-game).

**Is the plugin free of network access?** The plugin does not connect to the internet. Network access happens only when you press **Set up** (pip downloads) or when you set up the webcam tracker (pip downloads and the MediaPipe model files), in UDP communication on your own PC, and, only if you switch it on, in the touch events (a WebSocket server on `127.0.0.1` and webhook POSTs to this PC; other hosts only with `bTouchAllowRemoteWebhook`).

**Why are there no Blueprint events for the touch events?** The events describe where a person touches a character, so they leave the game as data you choose to read (WebSocket or webhook) and not as a game-side callback surface. The settings are plain properties and console variables.

**Why is there no https for the webhook?** The webhook is meant for this PC; `https://` would need a TLS stack in your package. Use a local relay if you must forward the events.

**Why localhost?** The stream says where somebody touches a character. By default it never leaves the PC, a web page in your browser cannot read it, and webhook URLs on other hosts are refused unless you allow them in the project settings.

**Can the tracker run on another PC?** Yes, with `UdpBindAddress` = `0.0.0.0` and a firewall rule on a private network.

## Support

* Support: https://github.com/DANTES-ZERO/softpoke-docs/issues. Please send: the SoftPoke and engine versions, your GPU and driver, `Saved/Logs/<Project>.log` (the `LogSoftPoke` lines), and for bake problems the files `manifest.json` and `quality_report.json` from the bake work folder. Do not attach models you have no right to share. The `softpoke-ops` skill can assemble a support report for you.
* Online documentation: https://github.com/DANTES-ZERO/softpoke-docs. Publisher: https://github.com/DANTES-ZERO.
* Support covers the unmodified plugin and the supported matrix (Windows 64, UE 5.8, D3D12 SM6, NVIDIA RTX validated, adult humanoid characters). It does not include custom bakes.
